In forty-one AD, a terrified middle-aged man hid behind a palace curtain, convinced he was about to be executed.
Hours after the assassination of Emperor Caligula, nervous soldiers swarmed the imperial palace quarters searching for remaining family members.
A guard named Gratus noticed two feet peeking out beneath a heavy textile drapery and yanked the fabric aside.
Instead of drawing a sword, the soldier recognized fifty-year-old Claudius, fell to one knee, and hailed him as Caesar.
Claudius, who had spent his entire life mocked for his limp and stammer, was carried to the Praetorian camp on a litter.
The guard unit effectively secured his loyalty with a massive financial bonus, establishing a pattern where soldiers selected the ruler of Rome.
