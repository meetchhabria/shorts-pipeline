In 1898, a Polish scientist in Paris worked in a leaky shed, stirring boiling vats of black ore.
Marie Curie suspected that the ore held an element nobody had ever seen.
She and her husband Pierre processed tons of it by hand, year after year.
By 1902 she had isolated one tenth of a gram of radium chloride, glowing faintly in the dark.
She went on to win two Nobel Prizes, in physics and in chemistry.
